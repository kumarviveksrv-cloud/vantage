All four stability checks passed (stable leadership, no restructuring, no culture crisis, priorities on track) → HCI 1.00x, no discount applied.
L1 · Value Ledger40
What it measures: the rupee return HR creates from retention, hiring quality, development and tenure, as a share of payroll.
18 Star performers, 55 employees with 5+ years' tenure, modest at-risk exits. The model credits HR with about ₹80.3L a year, roughly 5.9% of payroll. That's genuinely good, but still well under this scale's own ceiling (15% of payroll = 100), which is why 40 isn't a low score so much as "meaningfully positive, with room above it."
L2 · Talent Premium84
What it measures: whether promotions, manager effectiveness and hiring quality are actually working.
Of 20 people promoted in 12 months, 15 are excelling in the new role. 16 of 25 managers have outperforming teams. 24 of 30 new hires are meeting expectations. High ratios across the board.
L3 · Org Vitals100
What it measures: 21 yes/no questions on leadership credibility, psychological safety, manager quality and peer culture.
Every question answered "yes", leadership communicates directly, bad news travels upward fast, managers advocate for their people. This is the single highest-weighted pillar (30%) and it's doing the heavy lifting on the overall score here.
L4 · Human P&L86
What it measures: the L1 return divided by what the company actually spends running HR. Break-even is 1.00x.
Bright Peak spends ₹62L a year on HR (about 4.6% of payroll, above the typical 1.5–3% range, a deliberate investment), against the ₹80.3L return booked in L1. Margin: 1.30x, comfortably above break-even without hitting this scale's ceiling of 1.5x.
L5 · Human Balance Sheet74
What it measures: institutional knowledge and key relationships held (assets) against skill, growth and culture gaps implied by L2/L3 (liabilities).
Assets of ₹3.32Cr (senior tenure, revenue relationships) against liabilities of just ₹27L (L2 and L3 are both strong, so the gap-driven liabilities stay small). Net Human Worth: +₹3.05Cr, a real surplus.

L2 · Talent Premium44
What it measures: whether promotions, manager effectiveness and hiring quality are actually working.
Only 2 of 5 promoted employees are excelling. 5 of 20 managers have outperforming teams. 35 people hired in 12 months, but only 12 meeting expectations, a hiring volume problem as much as a quality one.
L3 · Org Vitals0
What it measures: 21 yes/no questions on leadership credibility, psychological safety, manager quality and peer culture.
Every question answered "no". This is the extreme case shown deliberately: the highest-weighted pillar (30%) at its floor, which is why the overall score here is pulled down hard despite L1 and L4 both still showing some real return.
L4 · Human P&L38
What it measures: the L1 return divided by what the company actually spends running HR. Break-even is 1.00x.
Oakridge spends ₹1.08Cr a year on HR, about 8% of payroll, nearly double Bright Peak's, mostly constant-rehiring overhead from high at-risk exits, against a smaller ₹61.3L return. Margin: 0.57x, spending more and getting less back.
L5 · Human Balance Sheet24
What it measures: institutional knowledge and key relationships held (assets) against skill, growth and culture gaps implied by L2/L3 (liabilities).
Assets of ₹1.09Cr against liabilities of ₹1.22Cr, the weak L2 and L3 scores directly inflate the liability side. Net Human Worth: −₹12.96L, a genuine deficit, what the company is exposed to currently outweighs what's booked to offset it.
What this is meant to show you
  • Same industry, same headcount, same payroll, ₹19.3L apart on HR spend and a 57-point Humac Score apart. The gap is entirely behavioural, not structural.
  • No pillar moves in isolation. Oakridge's weak L2 and L3 directly inflate its L5 liabilities, this is why L5 is described as secondary to L1, it depends on the other pillars rather than measuring something new.
  • L4's "break-even is 1.00x" only means something once you've seen a company land below it. Oakridge shows what that actually looks like: real return, exceeded by what it costs to generate it.
  • The HCI discount on Oakridge (0.85x) isn't decorative, it moved the final number from 24 to 20. It can only ever lower a score, never raise one.
  • Every figure above came from the same engine your own answers will run through. There's no separate "demo mode" math.
