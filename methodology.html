METHODOLOGY

The research
behind the numbers.

Every financial figure Vantage surfaces is either derived from published industry research, or explicitly flagged as a directional estimate. Not a proprietary black box. This page documents every source, so you can verify independently, and so you know exactly where an estimate ends and a data-grounded figure begins.

1. L1 VALUE LEDGER

Replacement Cost
by Role Level

When Vantage estimates the cost of losing an employee, it applies a tiered replacement multiplier (not a single flat number) because cost scales sharply with role seniority. These tiers are grounded in converging research across five independent sources.

Gallup State of the Global Workplace

Replacing an employee costs 0.5–2× annual salary depending on role complexity. One of the most widely cited figures in HR; used as the anchor range.

APPLIED TO → default replacement cost range
SHRM Society for Human Resource Management

Replacement cost band of 50–200% of annual salary; the widely-cited "6–9 months of salary" rule of thumb. Grounds the 0.8–1.3× tier applied to typical professional roles.

APPLIED TO → medium-criticality role multiplier
Center for American Progress 30 case studies · 11 research papers

The most rigorous cost analysis: up to 213% of annual salary for senior or highly skilled positions. Grounds the 1.5–2.2× tier applied to high-criticality roles.

APPLIED TO → senior/specialized role multiplier
Work Institute Annual Retention Report

Convergent research confirming that replacement cost scales with seniority, used for cross-validation alongside Gallup and SHRM.

APPLIED TO → cross-validation of tier shape
LinkedIn Talent Trends Global Talent Trends Report

Convergent on the same seniority-scaling shape: cost rises steeply for harder-to-replace roles, consistent with CAP and SHRM findings.

APPLIED TO → cross-validation of tier shape
Manatal Lower-wage role replacement research

Research specific to entry-level and lower-complexity roles, finding costs closer to 20–50% of salary. Grounds the 0.4–0.6× tier for low-criticality positions.

APPLIED TO → entry-level role multiplier
2. MERIDIAN CALIBRATION

India Attrition
Benchmarks by Industry

Vantage calibrates every analysis against the actual attrition rate for the user's industry in India, not a global average. The benchmarks below are sourced from India-specific surveys conducted between 2024 and 2026, with multiple independent sources landing on the same ranges.

Aon 32nd Annual Salary Increase and Turnover Survey

India-specific sourced ranges: Manufacturing 13–14%, IT/SaaS 12–15%, Banking/NBFC 20–24%, BPO/KPO 30–40%, Retail/FMCG 24–28%. Primary sourced data behind Vantage's highest-confidence benchmarks.

APPLIED TO → five directly sourced industry tiers
CIEL HR India Workforce Turnover Survey, 2024–25

India-specific survey data that independently converges on the same ranges as Aon, used to validate and cross-check sector figures.

APPLIED TO → cross-validation of sourced benchmarks
Business Today · WSNE Consulting · Venturesathi India-specific workforce reporting, 2024–26

Three additional independent sources covering India attrition, used alongside Aon and CIEL HR to establish national baseline and sector ranges from converging data points rather than a single survey.

APPLIED TO → national baseline (16–17%) and sector range confirmation
3. L3 ORG VITALS

Engagement Scores &
Manager Effectiveness

Two separate financial coefficients (one for engagement-linked productivity loss, one for manager effectiveness drag) both derive from the same foundational Gallup research program.

Gallup State of the Global Workplace
2.7 million workers tracked

Finding 1 (engagement): Top-quartile engaged teams are 14–18% more productive than bottom-quartile disengaged teams. Vantage applies 0.15× disengaged headcount × salary; this sits inside that real 14–18% range.

Finding 2 (manager effectiveness): Managers account for 70% of the variance in team engagement. A below-average manager driving roughly 50% of their team toward low engagement produces an expected drag of 50% × 17% = 8.5% ≈ 0.08× of team salary. Vantage cites this as a Gallup-derived inference, not a direct Gallup statistic.

APPLIED TO → engagement coefficient (0.15×) and manager drag (0.08×)
4. L2 TALENT PREMIUM L5 HUMAN BALANCE SHEET

HiPo Concentration
Risk

Vantage calculates annual expected financial exposure from high-potential concentration as an expected-value formula: flight risk probability × replacement cost. The previous flat 0.25× multiplier has been replaced with a grounded range.

LSA Global HiPo Retention Research

Up to 25% of high-potential employees expect to explore new opportunities within any given year. This is the flight risk probability used in Vantage's expected-value formula.

APPLIED TO → flight risk probability in expected-value formula
SHRM / Gallup Senior/specialized role replacement cost research

HiPos are by definition senior or hard-to-replace, placing them in the 1.5–2.2× replacement cost tier. Combined with the LSA 25% flight risk: 25% × 1.5–2.2× = 0.375–0.55× salary annual expected exposure per HiPo. Vantage uses 0.375× (conservative, active retention programme) or 0.55× (elevated flight-risk signals present).

APPLIED TO → replacement cost multiplier in expected-value formula
5. L1 VALUE LEDGER

Institutional
Knowledge Risk

Vantage flags knowledge loss as a qualitative risk, not a formula. No published research maps tenure-years to a salary multiplier reliably, and Vantage won't pretend one does.

Panopto / YouGov "Value of Corporate History," 2018

42% of an individual's institutional knowledge is unique to that person and cannot be recovered on departure. This grounds Vantage's qualitative risk flag: tenure is used as a proxy for knowledge depth, and attrition of long-tenured employees is surfaced as a concentration risk, not converted to a fabricated dollar figure.

APPLIED TO → institutional knowledge risk flag (qualitative, not a cost multiplier)
6. L4 HUMAN P&L

Legal Framework
for Compliance Exposure

Every legal citation in Vantage (across termination risk, POSH exposure, wage compliance, and criminal liability) is drawn from the primary legislation itself, not secondary interpretation. Vantage surfaces statutory text and penalty ranges directly, not paraphrased estimates.

Industrial Relations Code, 2020 India · In force

Consolidates the Industrial Disputes Act, Trade Unions Act, and Standing Orders Act. Governs terminations, layoffs, retrenchment, and dispute resolution. Primary source for wrongful-termination exposure modelling.

APPLIED TO → termination risk · tribunal escalation probability
POSH Act, 2013 Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act · India

Mandates ICC formation, inquiry timelines, and penalty structures for non-compliance. Vantage surfaces fine ranges, ICC gap risk, and inquiry deadline exposure directly from the Act's text.

APPLIED TO → POSH compliance exposure · L4 penalty modelling
Code on Wages, 2019 India · In force

Consolidates minimum wage, payment of wages, bonus, and equal remuneration legislation. Grounds Vantage's wage-gap exposure and bonus compliance risk calculations.

APPLIED TO → wage compliance risk · bonus exposure
Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2024 India · Replaces IPC · In force from 1 July 2024

Governs criminal liability for workplace offences. Vantage cites BNS section numbers and penalty ranges directly for criminal exposure scenarios, not IPC references, which ceased to be operative law.

APPLIED TO → criminal exposure modelling · compliance red flags
SOURCED VS. ESTIMATED

Where Vantage estimates, it says so.

Not every benchmark Vantage applies is independently verified by published survey data. Some are reasoned estimates, grounded in sector logic and structural arguments, but not yet corroborated by a citable study. Vantage applies two explicit tiers:

Sourced: Independently verified by multiple published datasets. Every source cited on this page falls in this tier. Outputs using sourced figures can state them with higher confidence.

Estimated: Reasoned from sector logic (e.g. "capital-intensive, low-mobility sectors behave like Manufacturing") but not independently verified. Outputs using estimated figures explicitly say so: they are surfaced with Medium confidence, not High, and the reasoning is shown rather than hidden.

Current examples of estimated benchmarks in Vantage:

  • Attrition rates for 12 of the 20 industry categories, including Telecom, Aviation, Oil & Gas, Healthcare, Education, and Consulting, are estimated from sector-logic comparisons to sourced industries, not independently verified.
  • Union tribunal escalation probability (25–65% range by union type) is reasoned from industrial relations structure: a recognised union already has standing grievance machinery while an individual employee does not. The specific ranges are not backed by a published statistic. Outputs say so explicitly.
